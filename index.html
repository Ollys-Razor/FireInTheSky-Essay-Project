One day, years ago, I sat, hunched over my phone. I felt buried beneath the enormity of the oncoming climate disaster, a disaster built of actions not taken, for decades gone by. Our leaders all denied or ignored the scale of the crisis. Such cowardice from the top sets an example. So I sat, and hid, amongst the petty diversions of the internet. It was in this state that I stubbled upon it: A song; to me it was a beacon. It carried a burning hope, pride, and faith in human achievement. Called "Fire in the Sky", it was about spaceflight; to me the meaning seemed much greater than that.

…Gagarin was the first, back in 1961 When, like Icarus, undaunted, he climbed to reach the sun…

In Greek myth, Icarus fled the Labyrinth and the Minotaur, flying on wings made by his father from wax and feathers. He escaped only to fly too close to the sun, his wings melting off of him as he plummeted to the earth. I was taught Icarus's story as a cautionary tale. The message was clear: don't ask for too much; don't fly too close to the sun. Yet here the song compares Yuri Gagarin to "Icarus undaunted". An image is conjured of an Icarus who, like Gagarin on the very first crewed orbit orbital flight, knew the danger he faced. An Icarus who flew towards the sun anyway. Here the story is not one of caution, but one of triumph; a mark set in myth that was not surpassed in history until Gagarin himself. Though Icarus fell, perhaps it was a will to find the very limit of possibility that drove him, not hubris.

…. we gave ourselves a decade to put fire on the moonAnd Apollo told the world, we can do it if we try

Here is the heart of the song: "we can do it if we try." If we set a challenge and choose to test ourselves against it, as president Kennedy declared in 1962, and we try with everything we have, as NASA did, then whatever that challenge may be, we can meet it and surpass it. The impossible was made real: in just 7 years from that speech NASA "put fire on the moon". If we could land on the moon within living memory of the first heavier-than-air flight, then every failure since is the result of insufficient leadership, will, and recourses, not the supposed impossibility of the challenge. Our leaders have failed to meet the climate crisis, not because they cannot, but because they lack the courage to meet such a challenge. This ignited hope in my heart. No matter how monumental the challenge is, if we devote ourselves and our resources, if we work together, and make sacrifices together, we can win.

…Yet the Gods do not give lightly of the powers they have madeAnd with Challenger and seven, once again the price is paid…

There is a cost to pushing limits. The hope this song inspires in me is not based on any certainty of personal comfort, or even survival. It paints the crew of the space shuttle Challenger, who fell like Icarus, wreathed in fire, to the ocean below, as heroes. The crew were engaged in a great challenge, one to which they committed themselves fully, and so their death carries a meaning just as great: don't give up. Disaster is no reason to live in fear.

… they passed from us to glory, riding fire in the sky… now the rest is up to us, and there's a future to be won…

I listened to that song several times before I looked up, returning to the room around me. It did not cure me of my malaise, but it gave me something to aspire to. Perhaps the song is only so great for someone like me, who already cared deeply about spaceflight, who grew up with astronauts as my heroes. But for me at that moment, the words of the song seared themselves into my mind. They echo there still, and likely will for decades to come.

"Fire in the sky" helped inspire a great collective hope in me. Where my old individualistic hope had made optimism for my own life hard, given the likely death toll of the coming decades, this hope lets me set aside my fear of my own future, in favor of a commitment and faith in the future of all humanity. I hear a message in this song that is not written in its lyrics. Maybe you can hear it as well: Have hope and have pride, for you are human, and as long as you have each other and a commitment to great goals, any struggle can be overcome. It is in such struggles that we are our most human. It is better to die like Icarus, reaching for the sun, than to live in fear of the minotaur.
